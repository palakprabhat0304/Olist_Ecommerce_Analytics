# Business story

## The question

Olist connects small sellers to buyers across Brazil. Where is the marketplace losing customer goodwill, and what could be done about it?

## What the data shows

**Most customers are satisfied, but not all.** The average review is 4.09 out of 5, and 77% of reviews are four or five stars. Still, 14.7% are one or two stars, which is 14,575 reviews.

**Late delivery explains a large part of the unhappiness.** Only 8.11% of delivered orders arrived after the estimated date. Those orders averaged 2.57 stars; everything else averaged 4.29. Nearly half of the reviews on late orders were one-star, against 6.6% for the rest.

**Delay length matters.** A delay of one to three days still leaves an average of 3.77. By four to seven days the average falls to 2.32, and past a week it stays below 1.8. The step from three days to four is where the damage is largest, which gives operations a concrete threshold.

**Delays are not spread evenly.** Orders crossing state lines are late 9.16% of the time, against 5.99% within a state. By destination, Alagoas (23.9%), Maranhão (19.7%) and Piauí (16.0%) have the highest late rates among states with at least 100 orders. São Paulo to Rio de Janeiro is the route with the most late orders, 1,264 of 8,188.

**Customers rarely come back.** Only 3.12% of customers placed a second order. The data cannot say why, but a late first delivery is one plausible factor worth testing.

**Payment is dominated by credit cards.** Credit cards account for 73.9% of payment records and 78% of value. Half of orders are paid in a single instalment, and higher-value payments tend to be split into more instalments.

## Recommendations

1. Make delivery estimates more accurate for the slowest states, rather than only speeding up shipping. A realistic estimate turns some late orders into on-time ones.
2. Track orders as they approach the estimated date and contact the customer before it passes, especially for routes with high late rates.
3. Treat the four-day mark as an escalation point for support.
4. Look at the seller and carrier side of the high-delay routes, starting with São Paulo to Rio de Janeiro for volume and São Paulo to Alagoas for rate.
5. Test whether a second-order incentive works better for customers whose first order arrived on time.
6. Keep boleto and voucher options available, since they cover about a quarter of payment records.

## Caveats

The comparison is observational. Product quality, seller behaviour and customer expectations also affect ratings. Complaint categories are keyword-based. The dataset covers 2016–2018.
